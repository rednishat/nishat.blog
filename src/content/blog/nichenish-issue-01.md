Hey,

So I decided to do something a little weird.

I already run three newsletters. And now I'm starting a fourth one to write about the other three.

If that sounds like too much, you're probably right. But here's why I'm doing it anyway.

I've been building newsletter projects in parallel for a while now, and most of what I learn from them ends up in my head and nowhere else. What worked, what flopped, what I'm trying next, why I made a specific call on positioning or format or platform. None of that makes it into the newsletters themselves because those newsletters have their own audiences and their own purposes. WP More readers don't want to hear about NishIsHere metrics. NishIsHere readers aren't waiting on my thoughts about HighDegree\* growth.

But somebody might want to hear all of it together. So that's what this is.

NicheNish is where I document what's actually happening across my portfolio of hobby projects. Monthly. Honest. No big lessons packaged neatly. Just what's going on.

Subscribe NicheNish, if you are interested in newsletters, hobby projects or just want to ride along with me.

## Newsletter #1 — WP More

WP More ([wpmore.net](https://wpmore.net)) is a WordPress newsletter targeting people who actually work in the WordPress space. Developers, agency owners, product builders, WP professionals. Every issue covers what's moving in the ecosystem: plugins, themes, builder news, community conversations, business angles.

It started as a small experiment. As of now it's at 316+ subscribers across 31 countries, which still surprises me when I look at the map. I've published 48 issues. The 30-day open rate is around 17%.

This one is the flagship. I'm taking it seriously in a way I'm not taking the others. I'm building out this one issue at a time. The next milestone is 500 subscribers by end of year.

The format is a specific thing I've dialed in over time: five articles per issue, each with an H2 heading, a short context paragraph, and a Read More link. Then three link sections at the end. The email title surfaces all five topics in the subject line itself because WordPress readers are busy and specific. They'll open if one of the five topics is something they care about.

I cross-publish it to Medium, and there's an audio version via NotebookLM at @WPMore.

## Newsletter #2 — Weekly NishIsHere

NishIsHere ([nishishere.com](https://nishishere.com)) is the oldest of the three. Issue 103 is recent. It's a weekly curated digest across Tech, the Creator Economy, Pop Culture, and Business. Four links per issue, a Thought of the Week, a Photo, and a Quote. 2,000+ subscribers.

This one I've been writing long enough that it runs almost on autopilot. The curation habit is baked in. But I've been rethinking the positioning this year because "curated finds for curious minds" doesn't actually tell a new reader who it's for or why they should care. The tagline describes a vibe, not a value. So I've been working on fixing the intro copy and the landing page framing without touching the newsletter content itself, which I think is solid.

There's also a Bangla version called NishNama Digest that goes out at my Bangla blog — [nishnama.com](https://nishnama.com). That's for a completely different reader, which makes it interesting to maintain both simultaneously.

## Newsletter #3 — HighDegree\* Marketing

HighDegree\* Marketing ([serplabseo.com/subscribe-highdegree/](https://serplabseo.com/subscribe-highdegree/)) is the most recent of the three. It's a weekly marketing newsletter built around three pillars: SEO, AI, and Social Media. Target audience is product marketers, marketing strategists, and SEOs.

At 28+ issues, this one is still finding its audience. The audience is smaller right now, but the goal is different here. This isn't a brand property the way WP More is. HighDegree\* is more of a credibility and personal brand funnel vehicle. The writing is more opinionated, more analytical. I cross-publish it to Medium and LinkedIn Pulse, and there's an audio version via NotebookLM at @LearnWithNish.

The growth has been slow but the open rates are decent. The posting schedule I've built around issue drops is what I'm testing most actively right now.
