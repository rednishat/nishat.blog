This was my first official Contributor Day. I had worked on Trac tickets before, but only on my own. This time I sat at the Core table and tried to do it the proper way.

[Bikash Pun Magar](https://kathmandu.wordcamp.org/2026/organizer/bikash-pun-magar/), from the website team, was the volunteer helping at our table, and he walked me through the process.

I spent the first part of the day researching how I could help on the tickets. What I had to offer was a marketer’s view. I shared my thoughts on four or five tickets, looking at what would improve the result for the person using WordPress.

One example is [ticket 65975](https://core.trac.wordpress.org/ticket/65975). The proposed helper text for the Site Icon said “square aspect ratio (1:1).” That is accurate, but I thought it could trip up people who are not thinking in aspect ratios when they upload an icon. In [comment 5](https://core.trac.wordpress.org/ticket/65975#comment:5) I suggested a plainer version: use a square image (equal width and height) at least 512 × 512 pixels.

People understand equal width and height without knowing what an aspect ratio is. “Use a square image” tells them what to do. And a simpler source string is easier for translators, which matters because this text is shared across two files and gets translated into dozens of languages. I also backed a follow-up Gutenberg issue, since the Site Editor words the same field differently.

The person who wrote the original wording replied. They agreed their first suggestion might be too technical for most WordPress users.

I liked seeing that. It felt good that my opinion mattered and that I could share what I know in a way that helped. I still get nervous when I contribute. But working on tickets alongside other contributors, I felt confident. I plan to join more Contributor Days and learn from the people around me.

The table itself felt different from working alone. People called out to each other. Laptops were open on the same ticket. Someone walked another contributor through a patch on their screen. By my rough count, there were more than a hundred contributors in the room. Between tickets, I asked people what they do and where they work, and those short talks made the hours go quickly.

I sat with the developers of [Codewing Solutions](https://www.linkedin.com/company/codewing-solutions/), a company run by [Nabin Jaiswal](https://www.linkedin.com/in/nabinjaiswal/). They showed me an integration they built with one of the tools I work with, and told me about a second one in progress. I talked with Nabin about it. He said that when it is done, we will do a collaboration to promote it. He also messaged me on LinkedIn offering help with anything I need. Nepali people are kind, and that is a big reason I like going back.

*I love you guys!*

## The sessions that stuck

On the conference day, I sat in on three sessions: Pooja’s, MD Saif Hassan’s and Shriram Lamichhane’s.

[Pooja’s session on WordPress education programs](https://kathmandu.wordcamp.org/2026/session/wordpress-education-programs-what-they-are-and-how-you-can-be-part-of-it/) gave me the full picture. I had not known how the education program and the Campus Connect and credits program run in practice. I also did not know the credit program certificate comes from the WordPress Foundation. If I remember it right, it carries Matt Mullenweg’s signature, which makes it more credible. The Q&A was useful. People asked how to sell the program to universities and how to show why the credit program matters. Pooja’s answer on selling it was that a university can add another credit program to what it offers without investing in building the workshop or the support materials.

<figure>
  <img src={img09.src} alt="Saif Hasan's Session - WordCamp Kathmandu 2026" />
  <figcaption>This was so good!</figcaption>
</figure>

I first met [Saif bhai](https://kathmandu.wordcamp.org/2026/speaker/md-saif-hassan/) at WordCamp Sylhet in 2023. His talk, “[AI as a Bicycle for the Mind](https://kathmandu.wordcamp.org/2026/session/ai-as-a-bicycle-for-the-mind-the-1968-idea-behind-human-centered-ai/),” had a full room, and people liked it. I did too. He came at AI from an angle I had not heard before. He went through history and said that at every big change, people claimed something was dead, and life found its way. His Jurassic Park framing of that stayed with me. His take on creativity was that creative people will thrive in the AI era. He also said we should use AI for augmentation, not automation. Augmentation means AI helps one person do more. Automation means replacing that person. We need to focus on this in our organisations and companies.

[Shriram Lamichhane’s talk](https://kathmandu.wordcamp.org/2026/session/how-a-16-year-old-built-a-free-classroom-for-millions-with-wordpress/) took me back to my early days. I also started by building websites in a cyber cafe, in 2005 and 2006. I built sites on Yahoo GeoCities and hobby blogs on Blogger, wrote posts, and tried to rank them. In the session, he said his platform gets more than a million visitors a month. What he built at his age impressed me a lot. I wish I could show his talk to many young people in my country.

There were [other amazing sessions](https://kathmandu.wordcamp.org/2026/schedule/#conference-day) too during the conference day. I wish I could join all of these too, but much of my time goes into talking and networking with people.

## What I’m taking back

- Apply to the WordPress education program through Make WordPress, as Pooja suggested, and wait for the lead to reach out.
- Explore a session at a school in Sylhet, which I raised with Pooja. I already talked about this with some of my teammates.
- Join more Contributor Days, and keep sharing a marketer’s view on Trac tickets.

If you are in the WordPress world in Bangladesh or anywhere, join your local WordCamp and talk to people there. It helps the open source community, and it helps you grow. Then try to get to an international WordCamp. It changed how I think. It will changed you too. You see the bigger picture, you get curious about more things, and that helps your skills and your career.

WordCamp Malaysia was my first international WordCamp, that experience was i thought the best. WordCamp Kathmandu is my 2nd international WordCamp, and it became the most amazing WordCamp i experienced so far! I am sure going to the next WordCamp in Nepal!

See you in another WordCamp! Peace!
